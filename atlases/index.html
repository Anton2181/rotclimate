Land and people around 200 AD

Map topics include Overview, Population, Foraging, Livelihoods, Political reach, Administration, Farming, Food and work, Health and ecology, Temperature, Water, Air and sea, Month by month and Fit checks. The Earth atlas opens on its Earth-corrected climate: every climate map, the station card, farming, population, foraging and political reach use the simulation after the corrections learned from the 31 Earth regions, with the raw simulation one switch away and maps of the changes under Fit checks (exact zone agreement 35.3 % raw, 38.6 % corrected). Food and work adds partial cereal field-work hours, cropped land per food target, reaping staffing for supplied harvest windows and calendar alternatives. These use the current crop model and Columella’s listed tasks, with explicit assumptions; they do not estimate total survival work or population. Choose the map view inside that topic. Administration compares seasonal two-day access from documented commandery seats, marked places, proposed towns, tracks, roads and military markers, with a separate three-seed settlement comparison. The workbook-backed inventory retains disputed seats. These scenarios measure access, not allegiance or military strength. Administrative links adds six schematic views of directed seasonal communication through 19 recorded provincial capitals, comparing marked interchanges with proposed transport. Reply budgets are adjustable scenarios, not limits that establish political control. Livelihoods adds worldwide usable forage, seasonal feed reserves and mapped water proximity, including land outside state claims. These are dry-matter resource scenarios, not herd or human carrying capacities; the zero-loss reserve experiment uses explicitly stated demand. Political reach adds Coverage audit: six views for all 18 mapped actors, comparing linked, claim-assigned and generated sites. Select an actor and travel budget to inspect seasonal same-site return access, overlap and missing evidence; this does not assign sovereignty. Sustained presence tests cereal provisioning, origin/destination stores and freight interruptions at all 151 verified marked sites. Livelihoods adds Seasonal circuits: four independent resource landscapes with a single closed herd route, finite carried feed and water-proximity sensitivity. Local and regional extents retain the same grazing footprint and travel budget, with nested candidate bases, an all-land fixed-base control and an optimistic resource bound. Origin storage and collection sensitivities expose food-support assumptions. These are explicit scenarios, not actual garrisons, camps, nutrition or control. Settlements and supply-supported reach now share one seasonal food ledger: local cereal caps civilian requests and available institutional cohorts, then changes which sites emit reach. A transport-matched unconstrained reference and legacy maps remain available. Three listed-field-work scenarios also cap cereal by annual/reaping budgets while keeping rural meals fixed. These are conditional scenarios, not validated populations or sovereignty. Overview holds the biome map, settlement potential, expected density and density around the old heartlands named in the campaign lore. Select any place for its climate chart, farming readouts and the real cities whose climate feels most alike.

Political reach now offers supply-supported scenarios: one grain budget supports requested civilians and institutional residents, and only available affiliated cohorts emit seasonal outward-and-return reach. Three listed-field-work cases also constrain the grain. These coupled scenarios remain uncalibrated for actual settlement sizes and sovereignty. Legacy views retain earlier source/reach rules and their historical Han/Roman comparisons; those tests do not validate the newer coupling. Trade routes bulk goods separately in each direction, with inbound hinterlands reporting people served rather than town inhabitants. The earlier Roman test did not establish that market access predicts town size. See the Earth evidence ledger for the distinction between measured inputs, empirical fits, literature relationships and scenario choices.

The Population topic is a lab: supply a total and choose even occupation, water and terrain, or market access. Six maps then show allocated residents, local pressure, spare food support, redistribution, walking to markets and settlement attraction, with province totals. The Foraging topic runs a hunter-gatherer scenario on each climate, adapted from the FORGE human model (published equations, with climate-based productivity and seasonal resource proxies in place of its ORCHIDEE vegetation), with coastal and freshwater fish as a third food. Its terrestrial and aquatic food scales were fitted on Binford's North American hunter-gatherers and tested on the rest of the world (density error a factor 1.8, rank correlation 0.74, and which groups fished, 0.78). Five maps show scenario density, daily food-procurement hours, days of food shortfall a year, the share of food from animals and the share from fish; select any place for its readouts. It is separate from the farming model and both draw on the same land.
