What the settlement map is calibrated against

The default Settlements map (release 3, sliding density, marketed share 0.39) stacks several layers. Count, positions, sizes and grain supply are each fitted on Earth data and scored on held-out folds, quadrants or halves. These tests are development tests on exposed pools, not blind validation. The open gap is Egypt: the harvest model feeds about half the Nile's historical population, so supply passes only without Egypt.

Positions

Production tiling under-filled the old heartlands; a heartland prior chosen on Han and scored on Rome (and the reverse) beat production in all eight quadrants. On Vidarbha in India, a survey region the rule was not tuned on, it neither helps nor hurts: 10 km capture +0.000, percentile +0.017 against environment only. With about 53 surveyed site cells, those percentiles are within noise of chance.

Sizes

Town areas follow the official Hanson 2016 table. Local people density explains almost none of the spread, so sizes are mostly a calibrated scatter around a weak trend. That is honest but limits what a map can predict about any single town.

Grain supply

The same production, freight network and allocation code runs on the Roman empire with real harvests. Without Egypt the cited 270 km haul limit holds and west and east agree. With Egypt it fails, and the cause is upstream: field-water work shows the Nile is now limited by the crop model's rain-fed constants (yield, farmed share, fallow), not by water.

Not yet calibrated

Reach, trade and administration are rebuilt on the calibrated towns but have no Earth test of their own. A floodplain crop rule is the next upstream step for Egypt, and it needs a held-out floodplain region before it can be adopted. No region has yet been held out blind.
