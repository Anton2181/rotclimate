Floodwater must survive until the crop needs it

Finite field storage is now coupled to the conserved river ledger. All 96 development scenarios are retained. Storage capacities are sensitivity limits; none has been calibrated or adopted for the settlement map.

The missing water is seasonal

The fixed November–March crop cannot use a summer flood merely because that water passed nearby. Water must be diverted once, retained in a finite store, and survive evaporation. Upstream withdrawals and delivery losses consume the same river budget; fields sharing a river receive proportional shares of available delivery.

For Nile barley with bare-field evaporation equal to reference PET and loss-free delivery, 100 mm storage covers about 10% of crop demand, 300 mm about 70%, and 600 mm all demand. The rain-only comparison covers about 10%. This shows storage sensitivity, not evidence that historical soils held 600 mm.

What remains before a map update

Both regions are development diagnostics. The Nile source is the qualified pre-dam Aswan gauge, but modern WorldClim climate is from another period. Every accessible candidate field is assumed connected to one optimistic shared river pool. Negative terrain height above the nearest river is retained from the groundwork mask; actual flood access and drainage are unverified.

The Mesopotamian test supplies only Euphrates water; it omits the Tigris and cannot establish a complete regional budget or non-inflation result. Field storage, bare evaporation and delivery efficiency remain uncalibrated, and groundwater, return flows and realistic travel/storage infrastructure are absent. Indus remains unscored.

Next: qualify field storage and access, establish a shared Earth/fantasy river-flow input and independent test regions, then test the frozen harvest rule. The current settlement atlas, marketed share 0.3923 and whole-empire Egypt failure are unchanged.
