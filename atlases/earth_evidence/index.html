Historical wheat: the average is easier than the seasons

Failed annual-skill checks. No production change. Broadbalk's unfertilized plot3 supplies actual harvested wheat yields, matched to observed Rothamsted weather. Twenty years (1879–1900) fit one yield multiplier; 25 later years (1901–1925) test it. This is temporal transfer at one continuously cropped English site, not a global or AD200 validation.

The selected multiplier is 0.933. Log error improves 6.4%, but predictions vary in the wrong direction and remain worse than the training-period mean. Multiplying yields cannot repair that response. A complete-temperature-record check (23 test years, no refit) reaches the same conclusion.

Winter seasons expose a structural limit: only 2 of 25 observed sowing-to-harvest seasons fit the current wheat length/temperature rules. 22 exceed the allowed season length; 15 fail the temperature window (overlapping groups). This monthly audit does not establish which missing process caused the yield errors.

Management matters: the test averages are 0.69t/ha without inputs and 2.16t/ha with 35t/ha/year of manure. Intensive organic manure is not generic ancient low-input farming. Nutrient depletion, weeds, cultivar changes and weather hazards are not yet identified by this experiment.

The initial complete-weather rule left too few training years. A disclosed amendment, frozen before numerical yield scoring, accepts published monthly means missing at most one daily temperature reading and retains the complete-record check. An impossible 1883 sowing date is excluded without silently repairing the source. Rain gaps are flagged; trace-only bounds are conditional. Woburn wheat/barley yields remain unscored until matching local weather is obtained.

Evidence by component

Climate physics

Earth-tested r43; dev49 and finer-grid checks; sealed e was scored and is spent. All unchanged coefficients are not automatically reclassified as fitted here.

Observable: Monthly temperature, rain amount/seasonality, SST; process budgets and resolution transfer.

Next: Audit remaining shared physics versus world-geometry fields; future heldout tests must respect the existing exposure ledger.

Crop production, area and calendars

Historical wheat test: observed-weather yield-scale candidate failed annual-skill checks (25 test years at one site, r=-0.378). Only2/25 recorded winter seasons fit current temperature/length rules. Prior physiological-clock candidate also failed. No AD200 yield default promoted.

Observable: Crop yield, cultivated area, dates and water response, separated by management and period.

Next: Chronological crop transpiration/soil evaporation/drainage budget and winter-season compatibility, constrained by documented management. Broadbalk now development/spent; Woburn reserved until local weather matching. See reports/HISTORICAL_WHEAT_20261105.md.

Soil water, runoff and seasonality

Simplified buckets and selected regional checks; no comprehensive discharge or soil-storage calibration demonstrated.

Observable: ET, seasonal soil moisture, streamflow, snow and low flows.

Next: Compare water budgets and discharge on Earth; separate measured soil heterogeneity from effective fitted storage.

Vegetation, forage and water access

Miami productivity and inherited allocation assumptions; seasonal proxy/resource circuits are not calibrated herd populations.

Observable: NPP, forage seasonality, energy/protein, sustainable intake and water permanence.

Next: Validate annual and lean-season resources before estimating animal products or mobile communities.

Foraging physiology and resource adaptation

Adapted kernel has continent-separated Binford tests; adopted aquatic calibration improves heldout error, with regional failures and later rejected variants.

Observable: Density, diet shares and procurement time; population dynamics and food balance.

Next: Keep geographic transfer tests; obtain independent prey/plant/fishing resource observations rather than fitting density alone.

Malaria and ecological population modifiers

Thermal trait equations are sourced; moisture gate and density penalty lack independent epidemiological/demographic calibration.

Observable: Vector/parasite responses, transmission season; separately illness, mortality and work loss.

Next: Validate suitability against appropriate pre-control data before fitting demographic effects.

Population allocation and livability

Historical analogue occupancies and HYDE/Han comparisons; scenario allocation is not an independent census forecast.

Observable: Absolute people and spatial allocation, with urban/rural distinction and recorded uncertainty.

Next: Use direct censuses/surveys as anchors; distinguish independently measured counts from HYDE imputation.

Travel and freight

Selected Earth comparisons and published/assumed rates; old market-access town-size test failed. ORBIS is a model comparator, not independent journey observations.

Observable: Observed travel duration, mode, cargo, monetary costs and seasonal restrictions.

Next: Fit transport to journeys/freight evidence before fitting trade outcomes; retain rejected scale identification.

Settlement sites and administrative seats

Seat-count/spacing and dry-site rules have Earth fits and later-era checks. These are not all-village or urban-size models.

Observable: Seat counts, site locations and independently estimated settlement size.

Next: Validate types separately; stop using served people as measured town residents.

Shared grain, residents and sustained reach

Conserved ledgers and sensitivity tests; cohort/request/storage/collection assumptions have not been empirically fitted.

Observable: Rural meals, stored grain, marketed supply, supported residents, documented institutional presence and access.

Next: Independent rural household/land/work evidence, then storage/imports and institution records. Never infer de facto control from painted claims used as inputs.

Shared rural workforce and listed field tasks

Normative Columella counts and scenario workforce budgets. The new modern reaping comparison failed its original panel-independence audit; only a restricted post-audit diagnostic remains, with no prospective qualification or AD200 adoption.

Observable: Reported task hours, cultivated hectares, household members and labour sources.

Next: VDSA manual-reaping benchmark with independent household size; modern evidence is not automatic AD200 transfer.
