Rotclimate Earth Test

The simulator built for the hand-drawn world was run, unchanged in structure, on nine real regions with their own terrain, Earth's tilt, spin and calendar, and scored against observed 1970–2000 climate (WorldClim 2.1). Then its physics was refitted on Earth and taken back to the map.

Nine regions, before and after

Climate distance compares every month's temperature and rain with the observations, cell by cell: 1 is about 2.5 °C or a 100-versus-144 mm month off. Two references come from the observations themselves: every cell given its region's average climate (no skill at all), and every cell given the average climate of its latitude band at its own height (a perfect model with no coasts or rain shadows).

Physics added to get there

Each piece answers a failure seen on Earth, is computed from the model's own winds, temperatures and coastline (no geographic labels), and is off by default: the live atlas's settings reproduce it bit for bit.

subsidence_drying

Dry air sinking in the subtropical highs replaces moist air, so the column dries without raining. Without it summer rain only waited until the water piled up.

sverdrup

Low-level flow toward the pole rises and toward the equator sinks (βv = f ∂w/∂z): dry summers on west coasts, monsoon rain on east coasts.

upwelling

Wind along a coast drives surface water to its right; where that is offshore, cold water wells up: cold summer seas off California and Iberia, mild winter ones.

continentality

The share of land the air has recently crossed, carried on the winds: sea air gives mild seasons, continental air harsh ones.

circulation_lag_days

How far the subtropical highs, storm track and monsoon lag the sun; it was fixed at 30 days and the rainy season began a month early.

land_heat_capacity

How slowly the land warms in spring; with the old value spring arrived a month early on every continent.

subsidence_width · storm_seasonality

Two constants of the old model made free: how wide the highs sink and how much the storm track weakens in summer.

lapse_rate

Fitted on Earth where heights are known: 4.4 °C per km near the ground rather than 6. Held fixed on the map, where heights are unknown.

The fit also had to be kept honest. Left free, it moved the subtropical high to 38°N all year, which dried the Mediterranean summers but killed the winter westerlies; bounded to Earth's observed circulation it kept the right climates for the right reasons.

Back on the painted map

A literature check on the cold-dry north: Earth's dry mid-latitude interiors owe their aridity first to mountains upwind and very long distances from a windward coast (Manabe & Broccoli 1990). Where neither is drawn, physics that works on Earth makes the interior subhumid, so that gap may belong to the map rather than the model.

Known limits

  • East China stays 3–6 °C too warm from autumn to spring, with too little monsoon rain: its Siberian winter air and south-west summer monsoon are continent-scale circulations a regional model cannot make.
  • Chile's coast is too warm in summer: a strip one or two 56 km cells wide against the Andes, whose cold marine layer is below the model's resolution.
  • Exact Köppen agreement in the Mediterranean regions is 22–42%; most misses are between neighbours such as Csa/Csb or BSk/BWk.
