Before the monsoon the model keeps Bengal under northerly winds and the dry descent of the tropical circulation (the e70b
branch drying, gated off only where it already rains, so a dry spring stays dry); the land overheats by 4 °C. The monsoon
then arrives late and its columns stay at 61-66 % RH, against near-saturation in reality.

## What it shows

The model's pressure field builds heat lows over the deserts as well as the monsoon lands, so a pressure trigger cannot
tell Bengal's pre-monsoon inflow from the Sahara's dry heat low. What differs on Earth is the moist southerly inflow from the
Bay of Bengal in April-May (sea breeze and the pre-monsoon trough), which the model's winds do not produce: the error is in
the spring circulation over South Asia, not in the rain-making. e80/e80b stay opt-in, default off.
