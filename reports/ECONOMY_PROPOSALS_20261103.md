# Next c.200 AD development round: from annual capacity to a seasonal food system

Prepared after the complete verified r43 atlas publication and the full
61-region storm-family comparison. These are proposals, not implemented or
adopted economic mechanics. The primary
recommendation is agricultural food stocks and grain flows, followed by labour.

## What is already present

The atlas has period crop yields/seed/fallow/irrigation, grazing allowances,
malaria potential, food ceilings and occupation scenarios; a HYDE-inspired
population-allocation lab; FORGE-inspired wild-food effort, shortfall, fish,
lifeways and camp moves; seasonal roads/river/sea transport, directed trade and
market catchments; effective political reach; and language/farming-arrival maps.
These are not all independent demographic predictions. In particular, market
catchment population is not city population and the current gravity flows are
not tonnes of grain. Proposals below target those missing links.

## Ranked proposals

| Priority | Mechanic | New maps or views | What it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Agricultural harvest calendars and grain inventories | Hungry season; minimum reserve; harvest surplus; storage requirement | Can a place feed people through the entire year, rather than only in the annual average? |
| 2 | Food-balanced town provisioning over the existing directed network | Import dependence; grain hinterlands; delivered food surplus; vulnerable supply routes | Which towns can be supported by this countryside, with these transport and storage assumptions? |
| 3 | Finite irrigation water and flood-recession farming | Water-constrained irrigable land; downstream stress; flood planting window | Does enough water arrive when crops need it, and who can use it? |
| 4 | Household labour, draught animals and intensification | Food-production work; peak labour bottleneck; non-food labour potential; land-limited vs labour-limited farming | How much work supports the food system, and when does work rather than land limit it? |
| 5 | Seasonal herds and transhumance | Winter/dry-season pasture; fodder deficit; herd movement corridors; water bottlenecks | Where can herders move to survive the scarce season, and how do cattle compete with crops and people? |
| 6 | Delivered fiscal surplus and state provisioning | Collectable surplus; capital provisioning; garrison supply range; seasonal tax/transport burden | What can a state maintain after the cost of supporting its subjects, collection and distribution? |

### 1. Agricultural food security and storage

Use crop growth and harvest timing on the existing climate; retain seed,
fallow, crop/uncropped land shares and period yield assumptions. Separate
harvest pulses from consumption. Carry stocks through the year with explicit
storage capacity, spoilage, seed reservation and an emergency-reserve policy.
Use daily or five-day accounting under the world's own 365-day calendar;
12 climate bins must not become twelve fictional calendar months.

A first version should provide a repeating-climatology seasonal budget. It
cannot estimate drought return periods or famine probabilities from monthly
normals. Scenario harvest reductions can be compared, explicitly without
pretending they have empirically calibrated frequencies. Storage loss needs
period-sensitive priors and ranges, not a universal modern percentage.

This is distinct from the optional food store already tested in the FORGE
adapter. That forager storage option was not adopted because it worsened the
fit; agricultural granaries should not be sold as a validated improvement to
forager density. FAO's post-harvest framework supports distinguishing harvest,
drying, transport, storage and processing losses; its modern practices are not
Roman/Han technology coefficients. [FAO, post-harvest systems](https://www.fao.org/4/ac301e/AC301e03.htm).

### 2. Grain flows and urban support

Retain asymmetric travel costs, seasonal sea/river access and the existing
road graph. Add commodity supply/demand: food left after rural needs, seed and
reserves; and separate town-demand scenarios. Route actual grain quantities,
with inventory and shipment accounting. Keep freight prices, physical losses
and route capacity separate. Cheap travel is not unlimited shipping capacity.
Initial outputs should show conditional provisioning feasibility and cost,
not claim a complete equilibrium price model.

This lets a town exceed its immediate cell's agricultural ceiling when its
imports balance an export elsewhere. It must not convert rural market
catchment totals into inhabitants of a city, reuse gravity weights as tonnes,
or add people merely by changing a route. Every case needs a closed food
ledger. Independent town-demand data or explicit town-population scenarios
remain necessary. ORBIS is a suitable historical transport foundation, not a
population generator. [Stanford ORBIS](https://history.stanford.edu/publications/orbis-stanford-geospatial-network-model-roman-world).

### 3. Finite irrigation water and flood-recession farming

The current crop model fully waters an irrigated share selected near rivers.
Replace that access proxy with a seasonal supply-and-withdrawal ledger before
calling irrigation reliable. Derive crop water deficits from the existing
water-balance calculation, restrict withdrawals to routed runoff available in
that season, carry downstream reductions and losses, and distinguish canal
irrigation from planting after natural floods. This connects directly to the
Egypt/Nile example without making hydraulic infrastructure automatically cause
a centralized state. Canal maintenance labour and allocation institutions are
separate inputs. [FAO crop-water balance](https://www.fao.org/4/x0490E/x0490e00.htm).

This has a prerequisite: audit river directions and contributing catchments
against the tiered terrain. The present political transport model's normalized
basin-flow index is not discharge in cubic metres and must not be used as one.
Coarse terrain cannot establish detailed gravity-fed canal feasibility; show
that uncertainty instead of inventing a precise water-delivery network.

### 4. Necessary labour and intensification

Add crop-specific land preparation, sowing, weeding, harvesting, processing,
water management and storage work. Annual labour availability and the busiest
few weeks are separate constraints. Draught animals save some human work but
consume pasture, winter fodder and care. Irrigation, manuring, terracing and
shorter fallow can raise production while raising labour or capital needs.

The direct output should be food-production work needed per adult and the constraint that
binds: suitable land, seasonal labour, water, animal power or food. A high
carrying capacity can coexist with a heavy workload. Food-production time is
not total subsistence work or leisure: fuel, drinking-water collection, care,
shelter and clothing require separate accounting before making that claim. This gives a concrete
answer to the original Marx/surplus-labour question without declaring that
one biome causes technological or political development. The archaeological
labour/land-limited framework offers relevant mechanisms, including a 2025
extension; it does not supply universal c.200 AD coefficients or a Gini value
from climate. [Bogaard et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.15184/aqy.2019.105);
[Bogaard et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2400694122).

### 5. Seasonal pastoralism

Replace annual forage allowance alone with seasonal accessible forage, water
and snow constraints, with optional stored fodder. Route herds between seasonal
pastures and charge movement/maintenance costs. Partition crop, grazing and
wild-food production to avoid counting the same NPP twice. This is herd
transhumance, distinct from the already mapped forager camp moves.

A simplified forage/grazing budget can draw on the process structure in
G-Range, without claiming to reproduce that model's vegetation/soil dynamics
or its modern grazing assumptions. Access rights and borders should be
explicit scenarios, not obstacles automatically inferred from climate.
[G-Range model description and evaluation](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/items/925ca946-63b5-4e11-9f33-db336319639d).

### 6. A state's fiscal and logistical budget

Distinguish total food from the fraction that can be stored, collected and
delivered after household subsistence, seed and transport. Compare commodity
and institutional scenarios rather than making rich polities automatically
stronger. Grain appropriability provides one published hypothesis; it should
be a testable mechanism, not an automatic state-formation score. Collection
rates, exemptions, coercion, legitimacy and institutions are independent
inputs or uncertain scenarios. [Mayshar, Moav and Pascali 2022](https://doi.org/10.1086/718372).

The previous state-strength multiplier failed independent tests. A new budget
should first map provisioning constraints and be assessed with independent
roads/cities and archaeological/documentary supplies, not fit borders using
networks placed inside the very polygons used as targets. Garrison reach
means supportable provisioning under a stated force/ration scenario; it is
not combat victory or a prediction that a particular empire must exist.

## A small reliability task before expanding forager demography

The existing FORGE-adapter report documents multi-year oscillations and
sensitivity to the averaging window. Check numerical time-step convergence
and separate ecological cycles from integration artifacts before calling
those variations a real population-risk map. Keep the successful fish/food
calibration and original results as controls. Do not turn on the previously
rejected storage option or smooth the output merely to make nicer maps.

## Delivery and atlas organization

Start with an agricultural stock-and-consumption kernel and one Food system
topic: seasonal security, reserves and net surplus as views, with the month
selector only where it is used. Add network provisioning next; reserve labour,
herds and fiscal scenarios for subsequent coherent milestones. Reuse the
existing view selectors and show storage, tax, mobility or occupation controls
only for modes that use them. Do not add a row of dozens of map buttons.

Every mechanism needs units and a conserved ledger, transparent parameter
ranges, a numerical control and prospective Earth evidence where available.
Do not infer weather probabilities from climatology or institutional choices
from biome labels. Retain potential natural biomes separately from human land
use. The resulting supportable populations are conditional scenarios, not
censuses; exports should distinguish production, actual allocated people and
maximum feasible support rather than silently mixing them.
