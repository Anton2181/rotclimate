# Overnight research batch — 15 October 2026

## Launch

From the project folder:

```sh
caffeinate -i .venv/bin/python -u scripts/overnight_v10.py --run --hours 9
```

The user requested an 8–10 hour overnight task. This command gives the experiment
a nine-hour execution budget, keeps the Mac awake, and writes progress/results
throughout. No further downloads or packages are needed. It is sequential, with
a finite work queue; it does not schedule a background automation.

The experiment's sources, reference data, four-layer profiles, settings and
selection protocol are already frozen in
`output/v10_overnight_20261015/manifest.json`. No seasonal simulation or calibration
was started by the assistant. The assistant ran fast tests, input validation and
52 single frozen-input operator checks (no seasonal integration).

## What the atmospheric audit found

At the lower offshore model level, the old prototype supplies roughly 60–90%
more moisture than the NCEP/NCAR reference. California winter mixing ratios are
7.21 versus 4.46 g/kg; Iberia is 8.77 versus 4.62 g/kg. It also misses strong
southward summer flow: reference northward components are -6.49 and -3.98 m/s,
respectively, while the prototype's mean northward component is zero. Summer
eastward flow is too strong. The assumed winter temperature profile is also
warmer than the reference, particularly aloft.

These are coarse reanalysis climatology comparisons, not direct observations
or proof that a single change fixes rainfall. See the completed atmospheric
audit in `output/v10_forcing_audit/audit.md`. Data are provided by
[NOAA PSL](https://psl.noaa.gov/); the [product metadata](https://psl.noaa.gov/thredds/dodsC/Datasets/ncep.reanalysis.derived/pressure/uwnd.mon.1981-2010.ltm.nc.html)
describes the 1981–2010 climatology, while the existing land/sea reference periods
remain 1970–2000 / 1971–2000.

## New mechanism under test

An isolated four-layer model in `experiments/v10_column.py` uses the same
approximate moist static energy and instant saturation/rain-out closure as the
previous heat experiment, with explicit water and static-energy ledgers.
Pressure mass fractions are 0.10, 0.15, 0.15 and 0.60; representative pressure
fractions are 0.95, 0.825, 0.675 and 0.30 of surface pressure. The upper layer
still spans a broad part of the atmosphere; this is a controlled prototype,
not a fully resolved atmospheric column.

Terrain-induced lower-layer slowdown is compensated in the adjacent layer
in the shallow-return version. That produces no terrain exchange above the
first interface. Representative reference heights are about 0.48 and 1.65 km
for the lowest two layers, so this avoids directly jumping mountain-affected
air across the previous 7.12 km gap. Matched deep-return and no-drag controls
are included. Full-depth synoptic disturbances remain a distinct forcing.

Reference wind vectors, humidity, temperature offsets and mean geopotential
heights come from the same declared offshore areas used in the audit. Profiles
are fixed from the 60 km input mask and reused at all finer/coarser resolutions.
Monthly mean vectors are spatially uniform within each layer; large-scale
circulation and momentum are still prescribed, not predicted. The wind/shear
and moisture changes are constrained by atmospheric data, not painted labels.

Water and static energy use the same dry-air fluxes and CFL limit. Positive
stores, layer/column dry-air mass continuity, boundary imports/exports,
evaporation, land storage, runoff and thermal relaxation are checked. Constant
latent heat is retained during condensation. Snow, cloud-water advection,
rain sensible enthalpy, water heat capacity, kinetic energy and a solved
radiation/momentum/ocean system remain absent.

Observed land temperature plus reference offshore vertical offsets are still
relaxation targets; do not claim independent temperature prediction. Reference
humidity at outer boundaries is capped by local saturation and the declared
humidity multiplier. Rainfall observations enter scoring only.

## Finite overnight workload

1. **Matched controls, 120 km:** thirteen settings in both regions. Separate
   changes to wind vectors, humidity and thermal profiles; compare shallow,
   deep and no terrain return, with and without storms. The legacy-like
   controls use the new four-layer geometry; they are not identical reruns of
   the original two-layer engine.
2. **Parameter coverage, 120 km:** 144 unique reproducible combinations in
   California, covering return depth, humidity scale (0.8/1/1.2), lower/upper
   thermal relaxation, storm rate, convergence, rotation strength, radius and
   terrain-drag height. This is a balanced design, not an unbounded optimizer.
3. **Transfer/weather, 60 km:** up to eight shortlisted settings in California
   and Iberia, using weather seeds 41, 73 and 109. Selection uses California
   screening data; legacy-forcing ablations cannot enter the shortlist by
   compensating rain errors with known wind/humidity/profile mismatches.
4. **30 km robustness:** up to six settings that qualify in both regions and
   all three checked weather seeds; extend to eight seeds per region.
5. **15 km resolution:** up to two qualifying settings, two regions and two
   weather seeds, if the budget reaches this stage.

At most 322 cases, each three to six repeated-forcing years; most queues will
not finish in one invocation. Each case repeats its fixed generated weather
year to assess settling. Different seeds are distinct artificial weather
realizations; warm-up cycles are not independent climate samples. California
and Iberia have already been examined and are development/transfer evidence,
not a newly untouched final test set.

The 120 km screen is deliberately inexpensive, leaving time for higher-resolution
checks. It is insufficient for claiming regional accuracy. Every resolution uses
the same 120 km scoring inset, raw input datasets and physical storm positions;
coastline/elevation sampling still changes. The 15 km check tests numerical
resolution using the same 10-arcminute input data, not new high-resolution terrain.

## Time, stopping and recovery

The nine-hour budget is checked within the integration loop. The last completed
month is committed using an immutable array plus an atomic pointer, so a crash
between writes leaves the previous committed month readable. An unfinished
month is recomputed. Completed years have checksums, budgets and evidence hashes.
Progress also survives the final-year-to-summary boundary.

At the time limit the script writes a report with status `budget_reached` and
exits; saving the final checkpoint/report can add a short overhead. Ctrl-C
requests the same graceful stop. Repeating the command verifies provenance and
resumes with a new nine-hour allowance. It refuses changed code, raw inputs,
prepared grids or checkpoints. Use a new `--out` for a changed experiment.

Five consecutive numerical failures stop the experiment for diagnosis, rather
than burning the remaining hours. Such a stop needs inspection before another
run. If all planned useful work finishes or no setting qualifies for fine-grid
validation, it can end early; it will not add idle time or refine rejected cases
merely to fill the requested window.

Regional runtime for the four-layer engine is not measured yet. The prior
two-layer coupled engine took about 18.8 seconds per simulated 60 km year.
More layers, faster observed upper winds and finer grids increase work; the
120 km screen offsets that cost. The work queue exceeds the likely overnight
capacity once refinement runs, while the execution budget bounds the duration.
Actual timings print every completed month. Allow several GB of output space;
a low-disk check stops before starting another case if less than 500 MB remains.

## Selection and morning outputs

Numerical water closure limits remain 1e-9 mm/step and 1e-6 mm/year; approximate
static-energy closure limits remain 1e-5 J/m²/step and 1 J/m²/year. Minimum
three cycles; compare successive years until settled or six cycles are reached.
Keep p95 limits of 1 mm/month and 5 mm/year rainfall, 1 mm water/soil storage,
and 0.05°C monthly/end temperatures.

Provisional screening guards also require lower/upper p95 temperature departure
<=5/10°C, regional annual rain ratio 0.5–1.75, model rain on at least 80% of
reference land with >=50 mm/year, and p99 annual rain no more than four times
the reference. This prevents an everywhere-dry model winning on average error.
These are declared development screens, not validated physical uncertainty
intervals. Monthly rainfall MAE ranks qualifying candidates. If no candidate
qualifies, the best corrected-forcing diagnostics can receive transfer/weather
checks, but failed eligibility remains explicit and blocks fine-grid promotion.

Look in `output/v10_overnight_20261015/`:

- `summary.md`: current candidate ranking, transfer/weather/grid checks and failures.
- `state.json`: precise stage, completed cases, shortlist and invocation history.
- `progress.json`: current case/year/month and update time.
- `run.log`: all progress and timing messages.
- `cases/`: settings, monthly resume points, yearly fields, physical budgets and summaries.
- `inputs/`: checksummed prepared coarse/fine grids, using existing raw data.
- `manifest.json`, `preflight.json`: frozen protocol and completed input/operator checks.
