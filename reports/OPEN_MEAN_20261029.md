# Regional mean throughflow — 29 October 2026

Allowing mass-balanced vertically averaged wind across the boundary reduces
maximum six-hour startup temperature adjustment in all three regions. It does
not establish improved monthly rainfall accuracy: rain is tiny and often remains
concentrated at the grid perimeter. No configuration is adopted.

## Physics and declaration

The literal setup was committed in `50e37eb` before the twelve integrations.
Both cases use the same archived simulated lower/upper wind shear, initial
temperature and vapor, Earth rotation, five-day drag, one-hour convective
relaxation, 224 km grid and six-hour duration. They use 900 and 450 s steps.
No observed wind, humidity or rain is an input.

One case removes the mean normal boundary velocity. The other retains the
source mean after an explicit global dry-mass correction. Their source
baroclinic shear is identical; this pair is a new controlled comparison,
not an exact rerun of the previous forced-upper-return boundary experiment.

Let `d_boundary` contain the actual column dry-mass divergence from prescribed
exterior faces. Interior velocities obey `D x = −d_boundary`, with global
`sum(d_boundary) = 0`. The model rejects net mass sources. The initial affine
projection minimizes the mass-weighted wind change needed to satisfy this
constraint. It can add or remove kinetic energy; the signed change is disclosed
separately, with no hidden heat compensation.

The diagnosed pressure multiplier now does nonzero work on the interior:
`work_internal = −pressure · d_boundary`. Its opposite is the outward boundary
geopotential work. The actual regional energy budget includes this term alongside
advected gas enthalpy, thermal geopotential flux and the work exchanged with
prescribed boundary velocities. Simply permitting mean flow without this pressure
work would fail conservation. Separate thermal/constraint geopotential terms are
saved, and the convection wrapper receives their total.

## Explicit boundary mass correction

Equal-width normal faces receive one uniform outward-speed correction at every
level, minimizing its kinetic cost and preserving vertical shear. The correction
is preparation, not a hidden adjustment inside the solver.

The large integrated mass numbers span long regional boundaries. Corrections
are small relative to the 5–7 m/s source flow. Corrected net transport is zero
to floating-point precision. Interior initial winds then receive the declared
affine pressure projection. Incoming temperature/q remain fixed at adjacent
initial simulated values; these are conditional, not transparent boundaries.

## Next physics gap

The regional domain can now exchange water and energy through a physically
accounted mean flow, but it still has no sustained surface/radiative driver.
A conservative surface exchange and radiation interface is a practical next
step toward integrations long enough to assess rainfall. Its assumptions must
remain separate from Earth validation; grey radiation, surface reservoirs and
bulk-transfer coefficients are approximations, not measured rainfall gains.

also remain unresolved.
