## Problem (r46, development regions)

- **Sahel–Niger:** rain peaks in May–June (127 and 119 mm) instead of July–August (observed 125 and 156 mm).
- **Najd:** June–September rain at 15–20° N is 243 mm, against 26 mm observed.
- **Algeria–Sahara:** false summer rain at 20–25° N.
- **Cause:** the land rain belt reached 6.7 + 15.8 = 22.5° N everywhere. That is right for India, under Tibet, but too far for Africa
  and Arabia.

## New physics: e85

The summer rain belt reaches further poleward where a high plateau lies directly poleward:

excursion = `itcz_shift` + `itcz_plateau_gain` × anchor, with anchor = 1 − exp(−A / 1.5 million km²)

A is the area of land above 2,500 m within about ±10° of longitude. This is the elevated-heat-source and orographic control of the
South Asian monsoon (Flohn; Boos & Kuang 2010). Africa has no plateau to its north, so its belt stays south. The Andes' Altiplano acts
the same way for South America. On the map, the anchor comes from the map's own terrain.
