# Revisiting de facto control and proposed settlements — 4 November 2026

Research proposals, not adopted mechanics or improved accuracy claims. Prepared against105bdff2 after reviewing the current control, settlement and de facto implementations and the earlier failed strength/market-access checks.

## What the current maps establish

`polity/fields.py` assigns places to the nearest polity network under a two-effective-day cutoff; a rival within half a day marks contestation. Roads and towns inside painted claims supply that polity's sources. Proposed unmarked towns are generated from modeled density, site preferences, spacing and a fixed stochastic seed, restricted to claimed land; they then enter the political source network and produce minor tracks. This is a conditional network-access model. It does not independently establish taxation, allegiance, military superiority, capital oversight or supply.

The settlement-count rule is substantially calibrated to administrative-seat counts, including HanAD2. Such counts are not interchangeable with all villages, commercial centres or city populations. Source types need to remain explicit. The failed market-access/town-size test in POLITY_V3_20261025 and rejected strength multipliers in POLITY_STRENGTH_20261031 remain negative controls; more taxable people must not automatically multiply political reach.

The annual metric is -D log(mean(exp(-monthly_days/D))), D=1day. As a mathematical illustration, two months at0days and ten at infinite distance give1.791759days, inside the2day threshold. This is not a measured case on the world map; it shows that the mean-reach score is not a guarantee of year-round connection. Existing worst-season/global accessibility layers do not replace polity-specific seasonal holding and supply diagnostics.

## Ranked proposals

1. **Separate kinds of power and keep the current map as an access baseline.** MonicaL.Smith(2005), *Networks, Territories, and the Cartography of Ancient States*, DOI[10.1111/j.1467-8306.2005.00489.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8306.2005.00489.x), argues that bounded, uniformly controlled territories can misrepresent ancient states. Glatz(2009), *Empire as network*, DOI[10.1016/j.jaa.2008.10.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaa.2008.10.003), offers an archaeological example of differentiated imperial relationships. Proposed views: administrative access, connected local authority, military-response access, supply reliability, and influence/tributary scenarios. These papers motivate a distinction; they do not supply universal control coefficients or automatically identify client states from geography.

2. **Model provincial administration and network continuity.** The2026 Nature Communications article *Network science reveals the structure and lasting impact of the Roman road system*, DOI[10.1038/s41467-026-75453-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75453-3), reports provincial capitals as key transport hubs in a large Roman road compilation. Our adaptation: centre→province→local-seat connections, with explicit delegation; roads transmit access but do not create independent governing institutions at every road cell. A detached provincial centre may retain local authority while losing imperial communication. Views: provincial hinterlands, isolation spells, dependence on bridges/ports/passes, effect of severing a corridor. The publication does not validate this command-chain model, and its aggregate road network must be filtered before using it as anAD200 snapshot.

3. **Separate settlement functions, then test a hierarchy model.** Bevan and Wilson(2013), *Models of settlement hierarchy based on partial evidence*, DOI[10.1016/j.jas.2012.12.025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jas.2012.12.025), and Davies etal.(2014), *Application of an Entropy Maximizing and Dynamics Model ... The Khabur Triangle*, DOI[10.1016/j.jas.2013.12.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jas.2013.12.014), offer spatial-interaction/settlement-dynamics challengers to a single spacing rule. Keep rural villages, market centres, administrative seats, forts and ports as distinct functions that can coexist at a site. Fixed marked places anchor the scenario; proposed sites compete for hinterland/services without creating people or food. Urban people must be drawn from a population budget; catchment customers remain separate from inhabitants. Views: likely administrative centres, market hierarchy, food-supported urban ceilings, site roles. These model families require full-method review and independent testing before parameterization; prior failure of our simpler market-access size model remains relevant.

4. **Make strategic reach depend on a conserved logistical budget.** Mayshar, Moav and Pascali(2022), *The Origin of the State: Land Productivity or Appropriability?*, DOI[10.1086/718372](https://doi.org/10.1086/718372), motivates distinguishing total output from collectible resources. Use that distinction, not a deterministic cereal→state rule. Our food ledger can test whether a specified fort/garrison ration and staffing scenario is supportable after civilian needs, harvest timing, granaries, physical losses and transport limits. Fiscal take, exemptions, cooperation and institutions are supplied scenarios. Views: provisionable garrisons, delivered surplus, resupply interruption, civilian burden. The existing route connector has departure throughput rather than a finite ship/vehicle fleet and holds monthly conditions fixed through a trip; these limitations must be resolved or disclosed before strong logistical claims.

5. **Show settlement and frontier robustness across alternatives.** Evans and Rivers(2017), *Was Thebes Necessary? Contingency in Spatial Modeling*, DOI[10.3389/fdigh.2017.00008](https://doi.org/10.3389/fdigh.2017.00008), examines how transport assumptions, model choices and uncertain site information affect which centres emerge. Our adaptation: rerun declared settlement seeds, plausible site choices, communication assumptions and source inventories. Map recurring centre regions and unstable alternatives; for political access show owner agreement and seasonal sensitivity. Scenario frequency is not an empirically calibrated probability, and simulated near-equality of travel times is not observed warfare or contested sovereignty.

6. **Validate urban populations separately.** Hanson and Ortman(2017), *A systematic method for estimating the populations of Greek and Roman settlements*, DOI[10.1017/S1047759400074134](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1047759400074134), offers a period-relevant population-estimation reference. Known archaeological settlement extent can inform historical comparisons; a campaign map's symbol size is not measured urban area. Suggested settlements without footprints need population scenarios/ceilings rather than an area-based estimate masquerading as an observation. Separate imputed/default ancient site areas from measured ones in validation.

## First implementation milestone

Before replacing de facto control, publish an **input-dependence and seasonal-access audit**:

* Compare marked sources only, marked+generated towns, and inclusion of generated tracks, holding climate and population fixed. Show where proposed settlement assumptions create additional political reach. Keep roads as explicit transport infrastructure; test autonomous road-cell source status separately.
* Compute per-polity months within the access threshold, owner changes, and longest consecutive loss-of-access spell. Keep annual mean reach, reliable access and garrison provisioning as separate outputs.
* Generate a small declared ensemble of settlement alternatives; report recurring centre regions and the spread of political access. Do not use the painted claim outlines to select candidate sites and then treat agreement with those same outlines as independent validation.

This first milestone is directly useful with available inputs and exposes existing assumptions. A hierarchical settlement/administrative model comes next, with food-limited garrisons after it. Intermediate models should be judged separately on settlement counts, withheld site locations, settlement ranks/sizes and access/control evidence, not only reproduction of historical polygon fills. County seats, forts, cities, broad claims and direct-control evidence are different targets. Existing Roman/Han/Tang comparisons have already been inspected and cannot be relabelled untouched tests.

No current map or coefficient has changed as part of this research proposal.
