# Herders vs farmers: Earth test of pastoral capacity, 20 November 2026

**Outcome: not adopted. The grazing pattern is flagged as unsupported.** On the Earth run, the model's split between herding and
farming barely ranks where societies relied on herding (blind Spearman 0.12). Scaling grazing up does not help.

Protocol: [plan](../calibration/herders_20261120/plan.json), declared before any score. Target: the Ethnographic Atlas (D-PLACE),
546 societies in the Earth run's area (0–56° N, 12° W–125° E), split by a 10° checkerboard into 268 development and 278 blind.

## Results

| | Development | Blind |
|---|---:|---:|
| Rank correlation, model herding share vs observed | 0.17 | **0.12** |
| Mainly-herders classification, current level (k = 1) | 0.671 | 0.704 |
| Same, grazing × 5 (fitted on development) | 0.723 | 0.692 |
| Mean herding share: model / observed | 0.12 / 0.31 | 0.14 / 0.31 |

- G1 (fitted level beats the current one by 0.05 on blind) fails.
- G2 (blind rank correlation of at least 0.30) fails.

## Why (development tiles only, after the blind run)

| Single predictor | Rank correlation with observed herding share |
|---|---:|
| Model herding share | 0.17 |
| Drier (lower rain over evaporation) | 0.44 |
| Shorter growing season | 0.52 |
| Lower plant growth (NPP) | 0.59 |

- **Wrong direction.** The model's grazing is proportional to plant growth, so it is largest where farming is also best. On Earth,
  herding takes over where farming becomes marginal: short or unreliable growing seasons.
- **Tsetse belt.** Hot, humid lands (growing season over 270 days, mean above 20 °C) have lower herding shares (0.19 vs 0.34).
  This is the tsetse effect, which the model does not represent.
- **Not like-for-like.** The atlas's herding share includes livestock on mixed farms (Eastern Europe 0.45, India 0.32). The model
  counts only grazing on unfarmed land. Level comparisons are therefore loose; the mainly-herders classification is the fair test.

## Proposed next step (needs a new blind pool)

Re-specify the herding share around crop marginality (growing-season length or plant-growth ramps) plus a humid-tropics livestock
penalty. Fit it on these 546 societies, now all seen, and test it blind on Old World societies south of the equator (Southern and
East Africa). That needs the Earth run extended south (`land_people_earth.py --window`).

Reproduce: `.venv/bin/python -I scripts/herders_earth.py dev` and then `blind`. The blind run has been done once; the checkerboard
blind tiles are spent.

## Re-specification v2 — ADOPTED (same day)

Plan: [plan_v2](../calibration/herders_20261120/plan_v2.json), declared before any v2 fit. The rule family (a growing-season or
plant-growth ramp, with an optional humid-tropics penalty) was chosen on the 546 seen societies and tested once, blind, on 145
Ethnographic Atlas societies of Africa and Madagascar south of the equator. The Earth run was rebuilt for that window with the same
code.

**Rule:** herding share = 0.207 + 0.331 × clip((240 − growing-season days) / 210, 0, 1). The penalty fitted at zero: the ramp
already reaches zero at 240 days.

| | Development (546) | **Blind (145, Africa south of the equator)** |
|---|---:|---:|
| Rank correlation, new rule | 0.54 | **0.42** |
| Rank correlation, old grazing share | 0.16 | **−0.06** |
| Mean share: new / old / observed | | 0.29 / 0.07 / 0.28 |
| Mainly-herders balanced accuracy, new / old (9 positives; reported only) | 0.74 / 0.69 | 0.65 / 0.55 |

Gates G1 (≥ 0.40) and G2 (≥ old + 0.15) both pass, so the rule is adopted.

**On the map:** a new layer, "Herders and farmers" (Harvest topic, raw and corrected). It is drawn from the map's own growing season
at ~27 km, as on Earth. Mean livestock share of food is 31%; 8–9% of land is above 40%; almost none is above 50%, because the
fitted ceiling is 54%. Browser check passes.

**Not changed:** the grazing allowance, food capacity and population. The per-polity herding statistics in the reach layers still
use the old share; they switch at the next reach rebuild (`polity/fields.py`).
