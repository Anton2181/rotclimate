# Polity v2: ranks, boats, seasons, refuge terrain, riders — fitted on Han China, tested on Rome

2026-10-25. Plan frozen before any result: `calibration/polity_20261025/plan.json`.
Code: `polity/network.py` (one month's graph), `polity/transport.py` (ships and river boats),
`polity/seasons.py` (snow, mud, heat, floods), `polity/control.py` (walking, riding, ranks),
`polity/fields.py` (the map), `polity/earth.py` (Earth windows and the historical data),
`scripts/polity_earth_check.py` (fits and tests), `scripts/plot_polity_earth.py` (figure).
Results: `calibration/polity_20261025/{sea,fits,tests}.json`; logs in `output/polity_earth/`.

## What was built

* **Ranked places (1).** Any place can start with a handicap in days (a virtual source node in
  the Dijkstra graph): a county-level place counts as if it were farther than a commandery seat,
  and a place can be worn down by its own days from the capital (Boulding's loss-of-strength).
* **Ships and river boats (3).** Sailing over every water cell by the month's wind: 5 knots
  before a fair wind, x0.7 along coasts, a square-rig polar that tacks at 80 degrees to the wind
  (dead upwind ~0.15 of the fair speed), the polar blurred where the wind swings (steadiness =
  |mean wind| / mean speed), light airs slow (Casson 1951). Boarding at ports (0.5 day), landing
  on any shore (0.25 day). The sea is *uncertain* (half speed) or *closed* (0.15 speed) by
  storminess, the swing of the wind about its mean, with thresholds fitted to Vegetius' sailing
  season. River boats on the navigable rivers: 70 km a day down, 17.9 up (ORBIS), upper rivers
  shut at low water.
* **Twelve months (4).** A degree-day snowpack and a 100 mm soil bucket (Hargreaves PET) give,
  each month: lasting snow (x2.5 off the roads), mud (up to x1.8 off the roads and on tracks,
  x1.1 on the marked roads; the bucket nearly full and the ground not frozen, or snowmelt), heat
  (marching shortened above 33 C), floods (fords off the roads slower when a basin's runoff is high).
  Year-round control is the mean over the twelve months.
* **Refuge terrain and riders (7).** A refuge factor exp(k x relief) on control; riding
  polities move at 50 km a day over open ground from wherever their herds graze.
* **Checks against Earth (8)** below.

## Earth data

Han AD 2: 1,294 seats with populations (1,196 county, 98 commandery) and the 103 commandery
polygons with the census (Zheng & Wu 2026). Rome AD 150: 1,353 Hanson towns, 14,616 Itiner-e
road segments (de Soto et al. 2025), the Cliopatria polygon (Seshat). Sailing: Casson's Table 1
(57 routes, both directions) and Pliny's record runs; ORBIS' network file (the route API is closed:
403). Winds: NCEP/NCAR 1981–2010 monthly means. Climate: WorldClim 2.1 5'. Grids: two 9 km
azimuthal windows (Han 378 x 400, Rome 400 x 600).

**What T1 says (post hoc).** The decomposition (figure, bottom left) separates three things:
the refuge factor costs most (best 0.78 with it, 0.82 without); the Han threshold does not carry
over (Rome is best classified at 1.5–2 days, Han at 5: the Han data has seats only, while the
Roman network, like the map's, has roads); and months plus boats together leave the Roman extent
where v1 had it (0.8204 against 0.8220 at 2 days, AUC 0.882 against 0.886). So on the map: held
stays 2 days, refuge is off, boats and seasons are on. The refuge slope in Han China is more likely
the Harvest model overrating people in the mountains than people slipping out of the state's reach;
it did not survive Rome.

**Sailing.** Distances and the eastern Mediterranean come out well (Rhodes, Cyprus, Crete,
Alexandria to the Levant within ~20 %); the model is ~2x too slow in the western Mediterranean
(Rome–Tarraco 14 days against 4; NCEP's 2.5° winds there are light) and makes the run home against
the Etesian winds too easy (Alexandria–Rome 32 days against Casson's 53–73). Against Pliny's record
runs it is 2.8x slower, as it should be slower than records, but by more than it should.

## Deviations from the plan (all reported, none of them tuning a parameter to a test)

1. Narrow straits (Bosporus, Dardanelles, Messina, the Gulf of Corinth, Euripus, Kerch, Bonifacio)
   vanish on a 9 km grid; they were carved as ferried water after the first sea run showed
   unreachable ports (first run: Spearman 0.69, median 1.38; `output/polity_earth/sea_run1.log`).
2. ORBIS' file numbers river and sea nodes as site + 100,000 / + 200,000; once matched, its river
   edges turned out to be upstream only, so downstream is a stated estimate (1 m/s current + oars).
3. The map's storminess comes from the open-ocean table (stormier than the Mediterranean at the same
   latitude), so F5's thresholds were refitted the same way on the table's 37.5 N row.
4. T8 crashed on a numpy scalar after T1–T4 had run; the rerun reproduced T1–T4 to the last digit
   and added the post-hoc diagnostics above.

## On the map

On by the tests: twelve months (snow, mud, heat, floods), ships and river boats, the sailing season.
Off: ranks (no gain on Han), refuge (failed Rome), riding (no polity named; see below). Held stays 2
days. Monthly winds come from each atlas's own simulation (`scripts/export_winds12.py`; the Whittaker
atlas re-simulated from its recorded parameters and seeds, its saved runs being of earlier fits).

New layers in Political reach: *Effective control* (now the year's average), *Control in the hardest
month*, *Months within a day*, *What closes the roads*, *Safe sailing season*, and ports and navigable
rivers on *Unmarked towns and tracks*.

Earth-fit atlas (a 34° tilt, so a hard winter): year-round control over the land averages 0.33, the
hardest month 0.25. The hardest month is January for 48 % of the land, February 36 %, July 8 %
(heat), March 7 %. What closes the roads then: mud 67 %, snow 20 %, heat 7 %, floods 1 %, nothing much
5 %. Most of its seas are safe 1–4 months a year (Vegetius' Mediterranean had about 3.5), longer in
the south. 222 ports, 1,089 river cells. Held areas move little from v1 except where the winter bites:
Xšāça 1,118 → 1,066 k km², Princedom of Olbi 55 → 40, Kingdom of Khurze 93 → 80, Avādāyā Tribes 62 → 51.

No polity of the map lives mainly by its herds (at most 0.09 of the food by people, 0.22 by area for
the Avādāyā Tribes), so even the post-hoc area rule would make none of them ride. Riding is set by
name in `polity/fields.py` (MOUNTED).

The other two atlases (with the E1 seat rule below): free fit, hardest month January for 62 % of the land
(February 11 %, July 10 %), closed by mud 56 %, snow 17 %, heat 11 %, floods 1 %, nothing much 16 %;
Whittaker fit, January 60 %, mud 47 %, nothing much 31 %, snow 13 %, heat 7 %, floods 2 %. All three
republished, with the hub.

## More eras and the steppe (plan: `calibration/polity_20261025/plan_eras.json`)

**E1, how seats are spaced.** Zheng & Wu's data also hold Tang 742, Song 1102 and Qing 1820. The number of
county seats hardly moved (1,294 / 1,560 / 1,287 / 1,948) while the recorded people rose from 58 to 351
million, so one seat per 40,000 people cannot be a rule for all eras. Seats per commandery modelled as
c × people^β × area^(1−β): in AD 2 the best β is **0.7** (deviance 256, against 449 for one per N people and
1,677 for one per fixed area). With the AD 2 constants and no refit, the mixed rule also predicts the later
eras best (summed deviance 5,803, against 6,598 for area and 12,229 for people), though its level drifts
(Tang under by a quarter, Qing over by 2.7x: Qing's 12 M km² take in the thin frontier). So the map's unmarked
towns now use it: one seat per 40,000 people at Han's mean density of 14 per km², fewer and larger towns where
people are dense, more where they are thin. Figure: `output/polity_earth/seat_rule.png`.

**E2, the drawn extent by era.** From seats alone, as for Han: the threshold that best separates each era's
prefectures from the land around them is 5 days in AD 2 (balanced accuracy 0.80), 5 in 742 (0.84), 3 in 1102
(0.88) and 5 in 1820 (0.76, China proper in the window). So a seats-only network needs 3-5 days, every time;
the map's network has roads, as Rome's did, where 1.5-2 days fitted best, and the map keeps 2.

**E3, riders at the steppe border.** The farming state reaches from its seats on foot (twelve months, boats);
the steppe polity from every cell of its polygon with at least 0.5 people per km², on foot or riding. Han AD 2
against the Xiongnu: walking and riding tie (balanced accuracy 0.967 each); Tang 742 against the Türgesh:
riding is worse (0.894 against 0.934). Riding lets the nomads hold far more of their own steppe within two
days (73-81 % against 52-66 %), but it carries them too far into the farmland (the Hexi corridor, the Ordos):
the border sat where the farming state's reach ran out, not where the horsemen's did. The plan's test fails,
so riding stays off for border contests; figure: `output/polity_earth/steppe_border.png`.

## Open

* Autumn mud (October–November rasputitsa) is missing: the bucket does not refill in time.
* The monsoon closure of the Indian coast needs more than mean wind speed (surf and onshore gales).
* The western Mediterranean sails too slowly with 2.5° winds; a finer wind climatology would test
  whether the polar or the winds are at fault.
* Herding polities cannot be told from their land's potential (Manchuria and the Ordos can be
  farmed): riding is a choice of the map's lore. Post hoc, the area-weighted herding share separated
  Xianbei (0.57) and Huns (0.71) from Rome (0.18) and Han (0.13), but oasis states scored high too;
  it would need its own test.
* Ranks found no support in the extent of Han China; another kind of evidence (the size of
  commanderies against counties, garrison reach) could still show them.
