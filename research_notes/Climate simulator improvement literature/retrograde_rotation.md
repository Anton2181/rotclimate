# Retrograde (reversed) rotation: what the literature says, and what it means for rotclimate

Scope: Mikolajewicz et al. 2018 (MPI-ESM "RETRO"), other reversed-spin and rotation-rate studies, where Mediterranean (dry-summer) and humid coasts end up under reversed spin, and whether rotclimate's "mirror Earth" treatment is adequate.

Primary sources read in full: Mikolajewicz et al. 2018 (PDF text, plus my own look at Fig. 10), Kamphuis et al. 2011 (PDF text), Seager et al. 2019 (PDF text) and Nakamura & Miyasaka (AMS conference paper). Other sources are from search snippets only and are marked as such.

Main URLs:
- Mikolajewicz et al. 2018, *Earth Syst. Dynam.* 9, 1191–1215: https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/ (PDF: https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- Kamphuis, Huisman & Dijkstra 2011, *Clim. Past* 7, 487–499: https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/7/487/2011/ (PDF: https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/7/487/2011/cp-7-487-2011.pdf)

## Q1. What did Mikolajewicz et al. 2018 find? Is the result a mirror image of Earth, or are there asymmetries?

### Takeaway
Reversing the spin hardly changes global or zonal means: global temperature is about 0.14 K lower and precipitation about 2% higher. It does mirror the zonal winds, the ocean gyres and the continental east–west climate contrasts, as Humboldt and Köppen would have predicted. The result is not a simple mirror of Earth's climate, though, because Earth's geography is not mirror-symmetric. Europe/Africa and the Americas swap climate characters (the Sahara greens; the SE USA, southern Brazil and Argentina become deserts). The AMOC collapses while a 23 Sv Pacific MOC appears. Europe's winters become more than 20 K colder. The storm tracks move from the oceans onto land.

### Cited Findings
**Setup**
- Model: MPI-ESM 1.2 in coarse resolution. The atmosphere is ECHAM6 at T31 (96×48 grid, 31 levels). The ocean is MPIOM (120×101 grid, 40 levels) with a sea-ice model. JSBACH provides dynamic vegetation and HAMOCC the ocean biogeochemistry. Forcing is pre-industrial (1850). The Coriolis parameter's sign was flipped in both atmosphere and ocean, and the Sun's diurnal march was reversed. Each run lasted 6,990 years and the last 1,000 were analysed. Ice sheets, greenhouse gases, aerosols/dust, soil colour and land use were prescribed at present-day/1850 values. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/)

**Global means and energy budget**
- Global surface temperature is 287.42 K in RETRO vs 287.56 K in CNTRL, about 0.14 K cooler. The NH cools by 0.55 K (287.21 vs 287.76) while the SH warms by 0.28 K. The tropics (22.3°S–22.3°N) warm by 0.45 K. Global precipitation is 3.11 vs 3.04 mm/day, and NH precipitation 3.18 vs 3.04. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018, Table 1](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
  - Conflict: popular summaries of the EGU 2018 poster quote a 0.18 K cooling. The paper's own text and table give about 0.14 K. — [EGU2018 poster (Ziemen)](https://mms.dkrz.de/pdf/talks/EGU2018-8161_Ziemen_Retrograde.pdf), search snippet only
- RETRO is "slightly cooler…, cloudier, and wetter", with about 1 W m⁻² more reflected solar radiation (higher planetary albedo). Surface moisture fluxes are larger and sensible heat fluxes smaller. The NH-minus-SH temperature difference changes from +0.57 °C (CNTRL) to −0.40 °C (RETRO). — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- Climate sensitivity is about 10% higher in RETRO (4.45 vs 4.00 K per CO₂ doubling), mostly from cloud shortwave feedback. Stratospheric water vapour is about 13% higher, and the cold-point tropopause is about 0.6 K warmer. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)

**Winds and temperature**
- "The trades blow from the west in RETRO and the midlatitudes have surface easterlies". The isotherms tilt the other way. The authors also note "second-order effects, i.e., deviations from a simple change in sign": the subtropical jets shift north (poleward in the NH) and strengthen slightly. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- "The eastern continental margins warm and the western lands cool". In midlatitudes the change is skewed: western cooling is amplified toward the pole and eastern warming toward the Equator. Strong warming occurs over SE Brazil, the SE USA (Atlanta) and SE China (Guangzhou). NW Europe's winters cool by more than 20 K. West Africa and British Columbia also cool substantially. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)

**Sea ice**
- Winter sea ice surrounds Great Britain and reaches the Bay of Biscay, while Newfoundland and Labrador become ice-free in winter. Similar changes occur in the Aleutian Basin and the Pacific sector of the Southern Ocean. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)

**Precipitation, monsoons and ITCZ**
- The warming SE continents dry out markedly, and rain moves to present-day deserts. Europe/Africa (especially Africa and the Mediterranean) becomes much wetter, and the Americas much drier. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- The Asian monsoon moves west and centres over the Arabian Peninsula. Ocean convection follows the warm water east, to the central/eastern Pacific and the SE Atlantic. The wettest place is near Ascension Island (8°S, 14°W), which gets a climate like present-day Palau. Tropical rainfall becomes "tripolar": the Middle East/N Africa, the SE Atlantic and the central Pacific. The zonal-mean ITCZ shifts south, and the Pacific double ITCZ becomes a single ITCZ. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- The east-Pacific cold tongue "largely vanish[es], rather than shift[s]" to the other side of the basin. Only a hint of a cold tongue stretches east from East Africa in the Indian Ocean. The main SST variability mode moves to the Indian Ocean in place of El Niño. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)

**Storm tracks (unexpected)**
- Storms travel westward. Their number, lifetime, growth and intensity change little (about 2 hPa more intense). Their tracks, however, move from the western-boundary-current regions (CNTRL) onto land in the NH (RETRO). Activity is strong from the Caspian and Arabian Seas through the northern Mediterranean, and north of the Great Lakes. The authors call the shift of storm activity from ocean to land one of the most unexpected results, along with a northward shift of extratropical storms and baroclinicity. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- Large moist-static-energy transport runs from the Atlantic toward N Africa and the Middle East, and from the Indian Ocean across India and Pakistan to the Mediterranean. This is the immediate reason the Sahara and Middle East green. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)

**Vegetation and deserts**
- Permanent desert area falls from 41.8 to 31.2 million km² (about −25%). NH deserts fall from 34.1 to 21.0 million km² (nearly −40%) and SH deserts grow from 7.7 to 10.2. Woody vegetation increases by 5.0 million km² and herbaceous by 5.6. Land carbon rises by 86 Pg C. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018, Table 2](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- "Southern Brazil and Argentina become the Earth's biggest deserts and the southern states of the United States see a dramatic climate shift from a fully humid climate towards a complete aridification." The Amazon shrinks substantially. "Many dry regions are simulated in RETRO, but extreme deserts – like the present-day Sahara – are less widespread." — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)

**Mirror symmetry vs asymmetry (the authors' framing)**
- In Köppen's idealized continent, "a retrograde rotating Earth would experience mirror symmetry about the north–south axis in its climate zones". "To a first approximation… these features do appear with mirror symmetry". However, "there are however some differences that would not have been predicted from just mirroring the idealized continent. Most prominent is the shift in deserts from the Eurasian–African continental mass to the Americas, with a greater center of mass over the subtropical southern hemispheric continents." — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018, Sect. 4](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- Conclusion: "deserts shift from the western subtropical continental boundaries to the southeast". — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018, Sect. 7](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)

**Ocean circulation**
- "What used to be a western boundary current in CNTRL becomes an eastern boundary current in RETRO". Strong poleward warm transport in the new eastern boundary currents warms those coasts locally; the western sides of basins cool. The ACC reverses to flow east-to-west. The Indonesian throughflow reverses (21 Sv from the Indian Ocean to the Pacific). The North Atlantic gyres weaken. The South Pacific subtropical gyre becomes about 2.5× stronger (over 130 Sv) because the straight western coastline of the Americas allows closed gyres. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018, Sect. 5](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- The AMOC (19 Sv in CNTRL) collapses. Only intermittent North Atlantic convection to 2,000 m remains, with a period of about 80 years. The NE Pacific forms deep water down to 3,000 m, giving a Pacific MOC of 23 Sv. NH ocean heat transport at 30°N barely changes (1.56 vs 1.52 PW), but the Pacific now carries about 80% of it. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- The mechanism is freshwater transport. The Atlantic north of 30°N becomes a 0.70 Sv moisture sink vs 0.10 Sv for the North Pacific, and transport across Central America now runs toward the Atlantic. The Mediterranean and Red Sea stop being net-evaporative and develop estuarine circulations. Atlantic/Arctic sea level rises by about 0.5 m, and the Bering Strait flow reverses. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- Eastern-boundary upwelling systems move to the western sides of basins. "The equivalent of the cold Humboldt Current flows northward in the western Indian Ocean along the coast of Mozambique". The tropical Indian Ocean becomes a 0.84 PW heat sink (vs 0.1 PW), taking over the role of the tropical east Pacific. A "super" OMZ forms in the northern Indian Ocean, denitrification rises about 50% (109 to 154 Tg N/yr), and cyanobacteria dominate there. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)

**Diurnal sun direction**
- An extra 6,990-year run, RETRO-S, flipped only the Coriolis sign and kept the Sun's diurnal march unchanged. Its differences from RETRO are "for the most part negligible", appearing only in precipitation: Africa gets 0.5 mm/day less, which is within interannual variability, and the Atlantic ITCZ shifts north. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018, App. B](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)

### Inferences
- The "mirror" holds for the mechanisms (wind belts, gyre asymmetry, which coast is warm or cold, wet or dry). It fails as a template of Earth's climate, because swapping east and west on real geography puts the large basins and continents in different places. For a single-continent model, this means rotclimate should mirror its physics, not copy a mirrored Earth atlas.
- Global and zonal means barely change. Rotclimate's planet-wide EBM, which supplies zonal SST and deep-continental temperature by latitude, therefore needs no spin-dependent changes. Only the east–west asymmetric terms should flip.
- The largest regional temperature anomalies in RETRO (NW Europe more than 20 K colder in winter, sea ice to Biscay) come from an ocean-circulation reorganisation: the AMOC collapse plus loss of the North Atlantic Current. A single-continent steady-state model with a parameterised `current_strength` cannot produce this kind of basin-scale switch.

### Gaps
- Fig. 10 (Köppen map) gives no area statistics for Cs (Mediterranean) zones under RETRO. My visual reading of the figure is in Q3.
- The analysis uses one model at coarse T31 resolution. The authors call for repeats with other models and higher resolution. I found no independent full-ESM replication.
- The Eos and DKRZ press pages failed to load (DNS errors), so those summaries were not checked beyond search snippets.

## Q2. Other retrograde / rotation-rate studies: what changes beyond mirroring?

### Takeaway
Only two other reversed-spin Earth simulations exist that I could find: CCSM3 (Kamphuis et al. 2011) and FAMOUS (Smith et al. 2008, an EGU poster). Both agree with MPI-ESM on the wind reversal, a much colder Europe, winter sea ice to Biscay and a weaker AMOC. They disagree on whether the overturning fully switches to the Pacific, so the ocean-overturning response is model-dependent. Rotation-rate studies (Kaspi & Showman; Way et al. on slowly rotating, retrograde Venus) concern changes in |Ω|, which reversed spin does not change. At Earth's rotation rate they imply no change in Hadley-cell width or jet latitude, apart from small second-order shifts.

### Cited Findings
- **Kamphuis et al. 2011 (CCSM3: T42 atmosphere, about 3.6° ocean, no dynamic vegetation; 500+500 years, only the sign of Ω flipped):** "the trade winds are eastward and the midlatitude winds are westward. The magnitude of the wind velocity is similar for PRO and RETRO." The east–west SST/SSS contrast within each basin reverses (a gyre dipole), and "midlatitude boundary currents… are located at the east[ern]" sides of basins. — [Kamphuis et al. 2011](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/7/487/2011/cp-7-487-2011.pdf)
- Kamphuis et al.: Europe is cooler "by as much as 10 °C" and winter sea ice reaches the Bay of Biscay, because "the main wind direction is now from the Eurasian continent, which has a continental climate". — [Kamphuis et al. 2011](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/7/487/2011/cp-7-487-2011.pdf)
- Kamphuis et al.: the Atlantic/Pacific freshwater-flux asymmetry reverses. Pacific E−P goes from −0.16 to +0.07 kg m⁻² day⁻¹ (net evaporative), and Atlantic E−P from 1.01 to 0.71. Trade-wind moisture transport over Central America turns eastward. Even so, the result is not an inverse conveyor. The AMOC weakens from about 18 Sv to 5–13 Sv, varying on multidecadal timescales, and the Pacific MOC rises only from about 7 to 10 Sv. — [Kamphuis et al. 2011](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/7/487/2011/cp-7-487-2011.pdf)
- Kamphuis et al. show that the geostrophic balance behind the MOC and the salt-advection feedback are invariant under rotation reversal. In other words, the internal ocean physics does not care about the spin sign; only the surface forcing pattern changes. — [Kamphuis et al. 2011](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/7/487/2011/cp-7-487-2011.pdf)
- Smith et al. 2008 (FAMOUS, EGU poster) found an inverse conveyor with strong North Pacific deep water formation. MPI-ESM's ocean changes are "more similar to the results of Smith et al. (2008) than to… Kamphuis et al. (2011)". Mikolajewicz et al. explicitly contradict Kamphuis's conclusion that continental geometry prevents North Pacific deep water formation. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf); [Kamphuis et al. 2011](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/7/487/2011/)
- Background mechanisms cited by Mikolajewicz et al. for zonal asymmetry: Seager et al. (2002) found that models without ocean-current asymmetries "see little change in the asymmetric continental climates", meaning the land's effect on atmospheric circulation dominates. Kaspi & Schneider (2011) showed that a simple heat-flux anomaly (cold continent air over warm ocean) is enough to set up the wind asymmetry. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018, Intro](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- Seager et al. 2002 (from the abstract and summaries; search snippet only): the main cause of Europe's mild winters relative to NE North America is advection by the mean (stationary-wave) winds, not Gulf Stream heat transport. Most winter ocean heat release is seasonal storage, not ocean heat-flux convergence. — [Seager et al. 2002, QJRMS](https://ocp.ldeo.columbia.edu/res/div/ocp/gs/pubs/Seager_etal_QJ_2002.pdf)
- Rotation rate (Kaspi & Showman 2015, idealized moist GCM; from secondary lecture slides and an abstract snippet): the Hadley cell widens as rotation slows and becomes nearly global at the slowest rates. Faster rotation confines it near the equator, with multiple eddy-driven jets. — [Kaspi & Showman 2015](https://www.weizmann.ac.il/EPS/kaspi/sites/EPS.kaspi/files/publications/Kaspi-Showman-2015.pdf) (not read in full)
- Slowly rotating Venus-like planets (Way et al. 2016; from the abstract snippet): with rotation periods longer than about 16 Earth days, convection fixed on the dayside builds thick reflective clouds that keep early Venus temperate. Way & Del Genio (2020) ran retrograde spin periods of −16, −64 and −243 days. These results belong to the slow-rotation, day–night circulation regime, not to Earth-rate reversed spin. — [Way et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.00706); [Way & Del Genio 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.05704) (not read in full)

### Inferences
- The robust, model-independent effects of reversed spin are kinematic: reversed zonal winds and storm-track propagation, mirrored gyres and boundary currents, and the warm and cold coasts swapping sides. The ocean-overturning response (AMOC vs PMOC) is not robust across models and depends on real-world basin geometry. Rotclimate does not model it and should not try to.
- Rotclimate keeps |Ω| fixed at Earth's value, so rotation-rate literature only confirms that Hadley-cell width, the latitude of the subtropical highs and the storm-track latitude should stay Earth-like. RETRO's small poleward NH jet shift is a second-order, geography-dependent effect, not a generic property of retrograde spin.
- If Seager et al. 2002 and Kaspi & Schneider 2011 are right that land–atmosphere interaction sets most of the east–west contrast, rotclimate's emphasis on flipping atmospheric terms (wind direction, thermal-wind turning, subsidence side) matters more than its ocean-current term.

### Gaps
- I found no "Liu et al." reversed-rotation study. No idealized-continent study of reversed spin turned up beyond Köppen's conceptual Klimarübe (redrawn in Mikolajewicz Fig. 9).
- Kaspi & Showman 2015 and the Way et al. papers were not read in full. The figures above come from snippets and secondary material.
- The Smith et al. 2008 FAMOUS result is known only from citations; it was an EGU poster, not a paper.

## Q3. For a continent at ~27–42°N with reversed spin, which coasts are wet or dry, and where do Mediterranean climates occur?

### Takeaway
Physically, Mediterranean climates sit on the poleward/eastern flank of the summer subtropical high, which lies over the eastern part of ocean basins under Earth spin. They also need winter storm-track rain arriving from the ocean. Under reversed spin, all of this mirrors. The summer high sits over the western part of each basin, so the continent's east coast at about 32–45° gets dry summers and wet winters (Cs), with hot desert (BW) equatorward of it on the SE coast. The west coast gets the warm poleward current and moist poleward summer flow, giving a humid-subtropical (Cfa-like) climate. MPI-ESM's RETRO Köppen map shows exactly this on North America and East Asia. That supports the calibrator's preference for retrograde spin given the painted warm-wet west coast, hot-dry SE and Mediterranean band.

### Cited Findings
**What Mediterranean climates need (Earth spin)**
- "Mediterranean-type climates are defined by temperate, wet winters, and hot or warm dry summers and exist at the western edges of five continents in locations determined by the geography of winter storm tracks and summer subtropical anticyclones." — [Seager et al. 2019](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10112964)
- In winter they lie "at the poleward edge of the winter Hadley cell and equatorward flank of the… storm track". In summer, "the midlatitude storm track is weaker and further poleward and each MCR is on the eastern flank of the subtropical anticyclones and hence under equatorward flowing, descending air (Rodwell and Hoskins 2001; Seager et al. 2003)". The west-coast position also gives "a temperate winter climate, free of continental cold air incursions". MCRs sit "between arid regions equatorward and more humid regions poleward". — [Seager et al. 2019](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10112964)
- The Mediterranean Sea region is a special case. Its summer subsidence lies "east of the North Atlantic subtropical high and more related to a forced response to Asian monsoon heating (Rodwell and Hoskins 1996, 2001)". Only Portugal and Morocco are strict analogues of the other four MCRs. — [Seager et al. 2019](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10112964)
- "Subtropical highs reside over the eastern portions of ocean basins throughout the year" and are stronger in NH summer. The high drives "a cool equatorward current off the west coast of a continent to the east". Strong northerlies on its eastern flank enhance coastal upwelling and evaporation, keeping SST cool. Cool SST plus subsidence favours marine stratus, whose albedo keeps the SST cool (a positive feedback). — [Nakamura & Miyasaka (AMS)](https://ams.confex.com/ams/pdfpapers/78520.pdf)
- A zonally symmetric Hadley theory predicts weak summer subsidence. Summer highs are instead explained as planetary-wave responses. Rodwell & Hoskins (2001) found that monsoon heating to the east (the North American monsoon for the North Pacific high, the Asian monsoon for the Azores high) plus topography are of primary importance, along with local cooling over the eastern oceans. — [Nakamura & Miyasaka (AMS)](https://ams.confex.com/ams/pdfpapers/78520.pdf)
- Rodwell–Hoskins mechanism (secondary summary): monsoon heating produces a Rossby-wave response with adiabatic descent to the west, and a Kelvin-wave response to the east that forms the equatorward side of the anticyclone. Radiative cooling of the descending clear air ("diabatic enhancement") strengthens the subsidence. — [Wikipedia: Rodwell–Hoskins mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodwell%E2%80%93Hoskins_mechanism) (secondary)
- Idealized-continent climatology (lecture notes): the driest subtropical regions are on the western sides of continents. In summer, winds are equatorward on west coasts and poleward on east coasts, where moist air circling the western side of the oceanic subtropical high brings summer rain. Xie and colleagues' GCM runs with an idealized subtropical continent gave an arid west and a wet east. — [UW ATM S 211 notes](https://www.atmos.washington.edu/academics/classes/2001Q1/211/notes_for_011801_lecture.html) (secondary, search snippet)

**Under reversed spin (MPI-ESM RETRO)**
- The "warming of the southeastern regions of the continents coincides with marked drying". The SE USA aridifies completely, and "deserts shift from the western subtropical continental boundaries to the southeast". — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- My own reading of Fig. 10a (RETRO Köppen map, T31 grid; telling the Cf, Cs and Cw greens apart is approximate):
  - North America: the SE USA from about 30 to 38°N is BW/BS (hot desert/steppe). The NE coast (New England, about 40–45°N) shows the Cs (dry-summer) colour. California's coast at about 35–40°N becomes Cf (fully humid).
  - East Asia: China's east coast from about 22 to 35°N is BW/BS, Korea/NE China (about 35–45°N) is Cs, and the interior is Cf.
  - Europe becomes Df (snow climate).
  - In CNTRL (Fig. 10b) the pattern is the usual mirror image: Cs on the California/Mediterranean west coasts, Cf in the SE USA and SE China. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018, Fig. 10](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- Under RETRO, warm poleward boundary currents run along the eastern sides of basins (continental west coasts), "lead[ing] to a local warming", and upwelling moves to the western sides of basins (continental east coasts). — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)

### Inferences
Mirroring the Earth mechanisms onto rotclimate's continent (27–42°N, retrograde) gives the following picture. This is my synthesis and has not been simulated by any study.

- **West coast:**
  - The ocean to the west has its mirrored subtropical high in its far-western part. Its eastern flank, next to rotclimate's west coast, carries poleward, moist, warm air in summer.
  - The warm poleward current runs up the west coast. This is the mirror of the Gulf Stream/Kuroshio off the SE USA and SE China.
  - Expect a humid subtropical, summer-wet (Cfa) climate. Under easterlies the west coast is downwind of the continent, so winters can be cold-continental, especially in the north-west. RETRO shows western-land cooling amplified toward the pole.
  - This matches the painted "warm-wet west coast".
- **East coast, about 32–42°N:**
  - Rotclimate's east coast is the western margin of the ocean to its east. Under retrograde spin that is where the summer high sits, with equatorward subsiding flow, a cold equatorward current and upwelling (Ekman transport goes offshore because it is mirrored).
  - In winter the easterly storm track carries ocean-born cyclones onto the coast.
  - Expect dry summers and wet winters (Cs) here, with hot desert (BW/BS) equatorward of it along the SE coast and inland. This matches the painted Mediterranean band and hot-dry south-east, as RETRO shows on the US and Chinese east coasts.
- **Monsoon-forced descent:** under retrograde spin β changes sign, so Rossby waves propagate eastward. The monsoon-forced descent should therefore sit east of the continent's summer heating, deepening the eastern-coast dryness. This is the mirror of the Sahara/Mediterranean link to the Asian monsoon.
- **Two conditions for an east-coast Cs:**
  - There must be enough open ocean east of the map edge to feed winter storms and host the mirrored subtropical high. The calibrator's land/sea beyond the east edge is therefore physically important for the Mediterranean band.
  - The east coast must not be shielded by mountains from the easterlies.

### Gaps
- No study I found has simulated an idealized single mid-latitude continent with reversed spin and reported the Köppen pattern. The east-coast-Cs conclusion rests on mirror symmetry plus a visual reading of a coarse T31 map.
- I could not tell exactly where the Cs/Cf boundary falls in latitude in RETRO from the figure (colour shades are close).
- Seager et al. 2003, Rodwell & Hoskins 2001 and Chen et al. 2001 (an alternative quasi-geostrophic Rossby-wave explanation) were not read directly. They are cited via Seager et al. 2019 and Nakamura & Miyasaka.

## Q4. Is a pure east–west mirror an adequate approximation for a small model, and what corrections does the literature suggest?

### Takeaway
For a single idealized continent, the mirror is close to exact physics. Flipping the spin sign and the sun's direction is equivalent to reflecting the geography east–west, and RETRO-S shows the sun-direction part is negligible. The non-mirror effects in the GCM studies come from Earth's real, asymmetric geography and from basin-scale ocean overturning. The correct rule for rotclimate is: "retrograde climate of map M = east–west mirror of the prograde climate of mirror(M)". It is not "the continent behaves like mirrored North America". The required corrections are to flip every east–west-asymmetric process consistently. Those are winds, the turning of thermal winds, which side gets warm or cold boundary currents, upwelling, the side where the subtropical high is strongest, storm genesis and propagation, and the direction of monsoon-forced descent. Gyre and current strength should also be tied to basin width.

### Cited Findings
- RETRO-S flipped only Coriolis and kept the sun's diurnal direction. Its differences from full RETRO are "for the most part negligible", at most 0.5 mm/day of precipitation over Africa, within interannual variability. The large changes are "ultimately dynamically driven through changes in the sign of Earth's absolute angular momentum". — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018, App. B](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- The Köppen idealized continent predicts mirror symmetry, and RETRO matches it "to a first approximation". The departures, such as deserts moving to the Americas and SH continents, reflect "the roles of the Americas and the African–European land masses" being exchanged. These are geography-specific swaps. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- The main ocean corrections that go beyond flipping winds:
  - Western boundary currents become eastern boundary currents and warm their coasts, while the opposite coasts cool.
  - Upwelling systems move to the western sides of basins.
  - Gyre strength depends on basin shape and width. The Pacific carries more heat "as the gyre transport in the Pacific is stronger than in the Atlantic, which can be explained by the larger spatial extent of the basin". The South Pacific gyre is 2.5× stronger with a straight western American coast.
  
  — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- The "cold tongue" (equatorial upwelling on the east side of the Pacific) largely vanished rather than moving to the other side of the basin, so not every feature mirrors cleanly. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- Storm tracks in RETRO moved from the ocean onto land in the NH. The authors flag this as unexpected and tie it to the different positions of western-boundary currents relative to land. — [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- Ocean-overturning switches (AMOC to PMOC) are model-dependent and driven by freshwater transport between basins. CCSM3 gave no full switch; MPI-ESM and FAMOUS did. — [Kamphuis et al. 2011](https://cp.copernicus.org/articles/7/487/2011/); [Mikolajewicz et al. 2018](https://esd.copernicus.org/articles/9/1191/2018/esd-9-1191-2018.pdf)
- Summer subtropical highs are maintained partly by local air–sea feedbacks: cool upwelled SST, marine stratus and radiative cooling. These follow the high to whichever side of the basin it sits on. — [Nakamura & Miyasaka (AMS)](https://ams.confex.com/ams/pdfpapers/78520.pdf)

### Inferences
**Exact symmetry (my derivation, consistent with Kamphuis's invariance argument)**
- The primitive equations are unchanged under the combined change (Ω → −Ω, longitude x → −x, zonal wind u → −u, meridional wind v unchanged), provided the diurnal sun direction is also reversed. So a retrograde planet with geography G is exactly a prograde planet with mirrored geography G′, viewed in a mirror.
- Rotclimate's mirror approach is therefore physically justified for its continent. "Westerlies become easterlies" is correct. "The continent behaves like a mirrored North America" is only a heuristic, because North America's climate also reflects Pacific/Atlantic basin sizes and the AMOC.

**How rotclimate currently implements the flip (`/home/user/rotclimate/rotclimate/model.py`)**

The following terms flip with the spin, consistent with the exact symmetry:
- `zonal_wind` flips u and keeps v.
- `thermal_wind` flips the cross-isobar turning angle.
- `sst` flips the boundary-current sign via `side`.
- The subtropical subsidence asymmetry flips via `basin_side`.
- The storm genesis asymmetry (`storm_asym`) flips.
- Orographic uplift u·∇h flips automatically through u.

**Corrections the literature suggests checking or adding**
1. **Boundary-current strength should scale with the width of the adjacent ocean basin.** Gyre transport scales with basin size, as with Pacific vs Atlantic in RETRO. A single `current_strength` applied equally to both coasts may be too crude. Under retrograde spin, the warm current on rotclimate's west coast is fed by the basin to the west. It should be weak if the calibrator puts land just beyond the west edge.
2. **Upwelling and coastal stratus belong on the east coast.** Under retrograde spin the cold equatorward current, upwelling and marine-layer cloud belong on the east coast at about 25–40°N. If the model only cools SST there, consider also suppressing convective rain and adding fog/stratus (a cooler summer maximum) on that coast. That is the mirror of California/Morocco.
3. **The summer high's strengthening side must flip, with its seasonality.** Under Earth spin the summer anticyclone is strongest on the eastern side of basins, partly forced by monsoon heating to the east (Rodwell & Hoskins). Under retrograde it should peak on the western side of the basin east of the continent (and be driven by heating to the west), and peak in summer. Check that the `subsidence_asym` term is seasonal (stronger in summer) and not just annual; dry summers are what define Cs.
4. **Winter rain on the east coast needs an easterly storm track arriving from the eastern ocean.** Storm "breeding" should happen over the warm-current side of the basin, which is the eastern side of a basin (continental west coasts) under retrograde spin. The storms then travel westward across the ocean to reach the continent's east coast. Fetch beyond the east map edge matters.
5. **The north-west should be the cold, continental winter side.** Under easterlies the west coast is downwind of the continent, so it gets continental cold-air outbreaks (mirror of the US/Asian east coasts). Western cooling should be amplified toward the pole, as in RETRO.
6. **Do not model ocean-overturning regime switches.** They are model-dependent and need a global ocean. If anything, treat the strength of the warm current on the west coast as an uncertain, calibrated parameter.
7. **Ignore the diurnal-sun direction.** Its effect is negligible (RETRO-S).

**On the calibration**
- Retrograde wins narrowly (F1 0.427 vs 0.421). That is consistent with the painted pattern (warm-wet west, Mediterranean band, hot-dry SE) being the textbook mirror of Earth's subtropical west/east-coast contrast. The near-tie may mean that the current parameterisation lets prograde runs fake the pattern through other free parameters (land/sea beyond the edges, monsoon strength).
- Making the asymmetric terms more physically tied to basin geometry, per points 1–4, may sharpen the discrimination between spin directions.

### Gaps
- No study tests the mirror-equivalence argument numerically on an idealized single continent. That would need two GCM runs (retrograde with map M vs prograde with mirrored M). The equivalence is a symmetry argument, not a cited result.
- I found no quantitative guidance on how boundary-current SST anomalies scale with basin width for a parameterised model. Only the qualitative RETRO statement (Pacific vs Atlantic, a 2.5× stronger South Pacific gyre) is available.
- It is unresolved how much of the east–west coastal contrast comes from ocean currents and how much from atmospheric stationary waves and land–sea heating. Seager et al. 2002 and Kaspi & Schneider 2011 favour the atmosphere, but those were read only via abstracts and citations.
