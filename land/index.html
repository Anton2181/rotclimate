How the numbers are made
  1. Monthly temperature and rain come from the climate fit of the map (the free-fit world), with the day/night swing learned from Earth.
  2. Evaporation demand: Hargreaves & Samani (1985) from temperature, the day/night swing and the sunlight of the world's latitude and tilt. A 100 mm soil holds water between months.
  3. Each staple of a Han and Parthian world (wheat, barley, foxtail millet, paddy rice, date palm) is tried in every season it could fill: its temperature window, its heat sum and its water balance (FAO-33 yield response; under a third of its water, it fails). Land near the rivers is watered: fully within a few km of a navigable river, partly along streams.
  4. Yields are low-input yields of the period: about 1 t/ha of wheat or barley, 0.9 t/ha of millet, 1.8 t/ha of paddy, 3 t/ha of dates; net of hulls, milling and seed.
  5. People = calories × arable share (by drawn terrain: lowland 85 %, upland 75 %, hills 45 %, mountains 8 %) × 60 % of that in fields × 68 % of the fields sown each year (all of them where a second crop fits) ÷ 1.1 million kcal a person needs a year, losses and draft animals included.
  6. The sown share is the one number fitted: run on Earth's climate, the same rules give the Han North China Plain the 29.4 million people its AD 2 census counted. Every other region below is a test.

It is a ceiling: how many farmers the land could carry once fully settled, not how many live there. On Earth, the old cores of the period lived near it; frontier provinces held a tenth to a third of it.

Where a region is known by its density, that density is applied to the land of the outline used here. The old cores (the Han plains, the Wei valley, Syria, Italy) lived near the ceiling; Egypt is above it because Natural Earth draws only two branches of the Delta, so most of the Delta gets no river water here. The Roman West, Mesopotamia under the Parthians and the Han south held a tenth to a quarter of what their land could feed.
